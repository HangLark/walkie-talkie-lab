# Generic narrowband FM software link

This is a real FM modulation/channel/demodulation calculation in software, not an RF transmitter, radio-hardware emulator, Motorola product model, P25 codec, or claim of perceptual equivalence. No radio hardware or network transmission is used. The analog receiver now uses this model; the operator sidetone and experimental digital branch remain separate. Historical analog audio effects are described in `CUE_SIGNAL_MODEL.md` but are not layered onto this link.

## Grounding and assumptions

The official [Motorola MTR3000 specification sheet](https://www.motorolasolutions.com/content/dam/msi/docs/business/products/two-way_radios_-_public_safety/base_stations/mtr3000/documents/staticfiles/r3-2-2010a_mtr3000_spec_sht_final.pdf) specifies ±2.5 kHz deviation at 12.5 kHz channel spacing, 300–3000 Hz transmitter audio response against a 6 dB/octave emphasis characteristic referenced to 1 kHz, and a 750 µs de-emphasis condition for FM hum/noise measurement. Those are generic parameter anchors only. This implementation's Butterworth response, matched-z emphasis, filter widths, squelch timings, soft limiter and receiver audio gain are design choices, not a measured MTR3000 response. In particular its two 300 Hz high-pass stages and two 3 kHz low-pass stages give roughly −6 dB at each band edge: that is not the specification sheet's response tolerance.

Receiver speech-band grounding also comes from the official [Motorola repeater specification](https://www.motorolasolutions.com/content/dam/msi/docs/business/products/two-way_radio_networks/repeaters/_documents/mototrbo_repeater_spec_sheet.pdf), which identifies 300–3000 Hz receiver response against de-emphasis, and [SDRangel NFM documentation](https://github.com/f4exb/sdrangel/blob/master/plugins/channelrx/demodnfm/readme.md), which documents an optional 300 Hz receiver high-pass. Our second-order implementation is a generic bandlimit, not their exact transfer function.

Primary technical mechanism references: [GNU Radio quadrature demodulation explanation](https://pubs.gnuradio.org/index.php/grcon/article/download/15/13/82) and [GNU Radio standard squelch implementation](https://github.com/gnuradio/gnuradio/blob/main/gr-analog/python/analog/standard_squelch.py). This module implements its own coefficients and noise detector rather than copying fixed-rate squelch coefficients.

## Signal path and units

1. Normalized mono audio enters at the host sample rate (8–192 kHz). Source leveling is an optional UI aid and defaults to zero. There is no automatic makeup gain, compressor, bit-depth reduction, pitch change, or artistic fading in the FM branch. Unity input peak at a 1 kHz test tone corresponds approximately to full rated deviation after band filtering.
2. Internal complex sample rate is `hostRate * ceil(48000 / hostRate)`, so it is always at least 48 kHz, even with an 8 kHz audio source. Low host rates use a precomputed 32-tap Blackman-windowed sinc polyphase interpolator. No arrays are allocated per sample. A matching FIR anti-alias output stage is used only when decimating. Its cutoff is 0.43 times host sample rate, avoiding accidental speech-band attenuation at 44.1 kHz.
3. A second-order 300 Hz high-pass and fourth-order 3 kHz low-pass bandlimit transmitter speech. The emphasis pole is `a = exp(-1 / (internalRate * 750µs))`. Emphasis is `(x[n] - a*x[n-1]) / ((1-a)*N)` where `N` normalizes the discrete response to unity at 1 kHz. The receiver uses the exact inverse one-pole recurrence.
4. Emphasized audio is limited to ±1; instantaneous deviation is `2500 * limitedAudio` Hz. Phase advances by `2π * deviationHz / internalRate`. Complex carrier is `cos(phase) + j*sin(phase)`, amplitude 1, power 1. There is no post-limiter speech filter; overload harmonics can enter the detector. This is an acknowledged transmitter simplification.
5. Independent seeded Gaussian I/Q noise is added **before** the receiver filter. A fourth-order Butterworth low-pass at 6 kHz processes I and Q separately. This is a simplified receive channel filter, not a recovered manufacturer's IF filter.
6. The discriminator is `atan2(imag(z[n]*conj(z[n-1])), real(z[n]*conj(z[n-1]))) * internalRate/(2π)` in Hz. The result is divided by 2500 and de-emphasized, then passed through a second-order 300 Hz receiver AC-coupling high-pass and fourth-order 3 kHz output low-pass. The RX high-pass also filters noise generated by the discriminator, which the transmitter high-pass cannot do. When both the carrier and noise are absent, phase has no physical meaning: the discriminator explicitly returns zero instead of interpreting tiny filter ring-down sign changes as an FM pulse.
7. Optional speaker/resonance/body EQ follows the receiver and defaults to an uncolored line output. A final safety knee is identity below magnitude 0.85 and smoothly approaches 0.96 above it. It protects against unsquelched noise; it is not a calibrated radio amplifier. Output gain and dry/wet comparison follow. The legacy final tanh/compression stage is bypassed for analog FM.

## C/N definition and quality control

`cnrDb` is carrier power divided by **total complex noise power integrated through this exact receive filter**, in dB. It is not RSSI, dBm, distance, sensitivity, or SINAD. The constructor measures the receive filter's discrete impulse-response energy `E = sum(h[n]^2)`. Complex noise equivalent bandwidth is `internalRate * E` Hz; each input Gaussian component has standard deviation `sqrt(10^(-cnrDb/10)/(2*E))`. Thus a unit carrier and 20 dB C/N produce mean integrated I²+Q² noise power 0.01 with the carrier absent. The finite numeric range is −60 to +120 dB; Infinity means no noise generation.

The UI mapping is deliberately uncalibrated: `C/N = −8 + 0.48*quality` dB, with quality ≥99.999 giving a mathematical noiseless endpoint. The default propagation mode keeps C/N static at fixed settings. Optional moving propagation applies a separately seeded complex Rician gain before AWGN; the setting then means mean C/N. See `FADING_MODEL.md`. The old correlated artistic audio-quality flutter is not applied. The old `noise` slider does not affect the FM branch. Identical input, rate, seed and control timeline produce identical audio independent of block partition. Different sample rates generate different noise realizations, not sample-exact resamplings of one realization.

## Carrier, squelch and timing

PTT/VOX creates or removes the modeled carrier; speech pauses do not remove it. The host's 24 ms voice buffer remains, with 20 ms extra filter-drain allowance before carrier removal. Source audio is not cut off at PTT release. FIR resampling adds a nominal 31.5 host-sample group delay at oversampled host rates (actual subphase emission shifts this by less than one host sample); the IIR filters add frequency-dependent delay. The 24 ms comparison alignment is therefore approximate and does not undo the filters' group delay.

Squelch observes power above 4.5 kHz in the **discriminator output**, exponentially averaged over 15 ms. Threshold is `1800*exp(-squelch/33)` Hz RMS; it opens below 80% of that threshold. Received complex power must exceed 0.01. A 16 ms acquisition interval, inside the 24 ms buffer, prevents false opening before a weak-channel detector has settled. There is 120 ms hold while carrier/speech is being delivered. At carrier removal a noisy detector gets only a 6 ms release hold; the actual tail can close earlier than the user-selected maximum `tailMs`. Tail noise comes from the same RF noise and discriminator, not a synthetic audio burst. `cueLevel` does not affect analog FM.

### Automatic squelch versus open monitoring

Automatic noise squelch remains the default. The new explicit `fmMonitor` boolean bypasses its mute decision during the current source/PTT/drain/tail window; it does not change FM modulation, noise, discriminator, or detected carrier. Manual opening fades over 16 ms to avoid a sudden gate step. `fmMonitorActive` and `fmRxOpen` are separate worklet meter fields from automatic carrier detection and transmitting state. It does not enable microphone headphone monitoring, start playback, key PTT, or produce endless idle hiss after Stop. This bounded transport differs intentionally from leaving a physical powered radio's monitor open indefinitely.

The [Motorola PM400 user guide](https://www.motorolasolutions.com/content/dam/msi/docs/business/_documents/user_guides/static_files/pm400_en.pdf) documents Open Squelch monitoring as distinct from automatic squelch. In the previous interface, setting the numeric squelch slider to zero still selected a finite 1800 Hz detector threshold; it never actually disabled squelch. The explicit mode fixes that omission. Its UI warns that low-C/N noise can be loud.

The near-threshold shortcut is now quality 35, or 8.8 dB mean C/N. The weak shortcut remains quality 30, or 6.4 dB. Automatic squelch may mute it completely; open monitoring exposes the same underlying weak waveform. At high C/N a stationary AWGN FM link really does approach clear speech plus approximately white de-emphasized hiss. Adding a true FM equation alone does not produce mobile fading or measured handset coloration. At lower C/N phase excursions and loss of intelligibility become nonlinear, which automatic squelch previously hid.

At `tailMs=0`, the gate fades over the final 10 ms of the filter-drain allowance and is closed before RF carrier removal. At noiseless quality, silent PTT start and end are exactly silent. Normal noisy tail output can be much louder than strong-carrier background noise, as FM carrier suppression is lost; its level has not been calibrated against a physical receiver. The host's 350 ms export drain includes the voice buffer, filter drain, maximum 200 ms tail, and gate release.

## Verification and limits

- `tests/analog-fm.test.mjs`: physical phase/deviation units, clean 1 kHz roundtrip, response consistency at 44.1/48/96/192 kHz, actual noise scaling and threshold behavior, carrier versus silence detector, finite extreme inputs/CNR/rates, seed and partition invariance, short onset/sibilant release.
- New monitor/RX-filter tests verify phase excursions increase near threshold, receiver DC rejection and sub-300 Hz noise reduction, unchanged detector input, exact underlying FM sequence under auto/manual gating, 16 ms gate smoothing, no idle/start side effect, finite release, and unchanged operator/digital routing. Independent QA compared 1,454,760 underlying phase/discriminator samples exactly across both gate modes at six sample rates and six quality levels.
- `tests/fm-integration.test.mjs`: no undefined-phase release clicks; zero-tail preclosure; bounded noisy tails; weak-channel startup; selector changes during a draining burst; early onset and late sibilant preservation; re-keying and sample/block parity. Existing cue tests now test real carrier behavior while retaining exact operator permit expectations.
- Independent checks found and fixed excess 44.1 kHz decimator rolloff, undefined noiseless carrier-off phase clicks, weak-channel false acquisition, and switching receiver algorithms during a queued release.
- Independent integrated-filter noise measurements at 20 dB C/N across 8/44.1/48/96/192 kHz were 0.00995–0.01012 versus expected 0.01. After the receiver high-pass correction, clean response at 100/300/1000/2000/3000 Hz is approximately −38.28/−6.02/−0.071/−0.325/−6.08 dB at 48 kHz. At 0 dB C/N, the fraction of output noise power below 300 Hz fell from 63.3% to 15.8% and RMS fell from 0.947 to 0.635; at 30 dB the fraction fell from 3.56% to 0.89%. These are measured model outputs, not a hardware trace.
- Before the receiver high-pass correction, actual clean CMU ARCTIC SLT/BDL human fixtures were run at 16 kHz unity input through the standalone noiseless model: output peaks 0.815/0.766 and RMS 0.0836/0.0665. SLT reached the deviation limiter on 2 of 165041 observed host samples; BDL on 0 of 159603. These are numerical checks, not listening or hardware-fidelity evidence.
- `node scripts/benchmark-fm.mjs` measures Node throughput, not browser realtime reliability. One cloud run measured 10–34 ms per audio-second at 8–192 kHz. Browser AudioWorklet performance, microphone hardware coloration and actual radio equivalence require separate verification.

Not modeled: measured microphone/speaker impulse responses, RF front-end nonlinearities, adjacent/co-channel interferers or capture effect, frequency-selective delayed multipath, measured/calibrated fading, PLL/AFC, actual manufacturer limiter or squelch circuits, CTCSS/DCS, or any digital voice codec. No claim of perceptual realism follows from passing these tests. A same-utterance physical-radio reference and controlled listening evaluation are still needed for that claim.

## Listening checks for the monitor correction

Use the same bundled human utterance and playback level, initially low. At strong quality 90 (35.2 dB C/N), opening squelch should not substantially change the established voice/noise. At the 8.8 dB threshold shortcut, speech and phase-noise impairment are audible. At quality 30 (6.4 dB), compare automatic squelch (possibly fully silent) with explicit open monitoring (weak speech/noise remains audible). At still lower C/N intelligibility falls further. Turn monitoring off to compare the receiver's intentional muting. Stop must still end output; changing this setting never starts a microphone or playback.

The user's listening feedback established that the original static model sounded too much like voice plus white noise and abruptly became silent. This correction exposes the previously hidden threshold regime and fixes receiver sub-audio filtering. It is not a claim that physical handheld-radio realism has been achieved. Optional unresolved multipath/fading is implemented in the separately reviewed model described in `FADING_MODEL.md`; its physical assumptions and listening limitations remain explicit.
